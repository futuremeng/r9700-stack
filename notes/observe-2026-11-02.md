**Reading.** Aggregate generation never leaves 39–64 tok/s while first-token p50 walks 5× and per-request decode
falls 13×. Past the prefill pipe's ceiling, concurrency is not bought with throughput — it is paid for in
latency, and the aggregate number alone hides that, which is why the buckets carry busy streams and both rates.
Aggregate prefill measured from the engine's own row timings: 6,410,492 prompt tokens / 839 s = **7,638 tok/s**,
inside the 6.8–9.1k band the synthetic ladder in `notes/context-262k.md` measured, so nothing new about the
pipe — the shape of the arrival pattern is new (mixed lengths hold 4–7 running where synthetic ≥30k loads peaked
at 2, because short requests slip between the long prefills).

**Engine step cadence:** p50 951–1010 ms = one 8192-token chunk at that prefill rate. Consequence, and a trap in
the Monitor: vLLM credits generation tokens **per engine step**, so a 1 s sampler aliases against a ~1 s step —
`stream_tok_s_live` reads p50 1.8 tok/s while the same requests average 33 tok/s end to end. The per-second
sparkline is an activity trace, not a quotable rate.

## 14:30–14:52 — why does card4 sit hotter? (measured; answer is "it isn't the card that is hotter, it is its memory")

Read directly from `/sys/class/drm/cardN/device/hwmon/hwmon*/` at 1 Hz for 180 s while traffic ran (note: three
temperature sensors per card, labelled **edge**, **junction**, **mem** — the Monitor had been reading `temp1`,
i.e. `edge`, only). A first pass of this sampler reported all four cards identical — a late-bound closure made
every card read the last card's hwmon; fixed by binding `hm` per iteration.

| card | PCI | W avg (range) | edge °C | junction °C | **mem °C** | fan rpm | pwm | sclk avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| card1 | `c3:00.0` | 293 (216–423) | 68.0 (67–69) | **89.7** (82–93) | 84.8 (82–86) | 4006 | 180 | 2876 MHz |
| card2 | `c6:00.0` | 291 (210–326) | 67.8 (67–68) | **102.4** (92–106) | 86.0 (84–88) | 4652 | 192 | 2836 MHz |
| card3 | `03:00.0` | 291 (213–328) | 66.2 (65–67) | 99.4 (90–103) | 82.7 (80–84) | 4496 | 193 | 2952 MHz |
| card4 | `06:00.0` | 293 (215–330) | **77.3** (77–78) | 99.1 (92–102) | **95.7** (93–97) | 4472 | 187 | 2820 MHz |

**What is ruled out by this table:**

- **Not the card's work.** All four draw 291–293 W at the same clocks (2820–2952 MHz) — TP4 splits it evenly.
- **Not the die's cooling.** card4's junction (99.1 °C) is indistinguishable from card3's (99.4) at equal power.
- **Not hardware identity.** All four are the same SKU and revision (`1002:7551`, subsystem `1efe:0058`, rev
  `c0`), same VBIOS (`113-EXT127429-001`), same memory clock (1258 MHz), same `power1_cap` (300 W), same
  `temp1_crit` (110 °C).
- **Not a sensor calibration offset.** Idle readings (earlier the same day, serve down or idle) had all four edge
  sensors inside ~31–36 °C, card4 with the pack; the gap appears only under load.

**What is left, and it is a different fault than the one the Monitor implied:** card4's anomaly is in **edge
(+9 to +11 °C)** and **GDDR7 memory (+10 to +13 °C)** while its junction is normal. The heat is in the board and
memory region, not the core. Two candidates survive:

1. **Local airflow over that card's memory side** — card4 hangs off a different switch group (`06:00.0 → 05:00.0 →
   04:00.0 → 00:03.1`) than card2 (`c6 → c5 → c4 → c0:03.1`); a bay/blanking or recirculation difference between
   those two groups is software-invisible. Against this: if card4 sat in pre-heated intake air, its **junction**
   would rise too (the die's path to ambient is the dominant one) and the fan would answer; instead its junction
   matches card3's and its fan duty is *lower* (pwm 187 vs 192/193).
2. **card4's memory-side thermal interface** — pads/heatsink contact on the memory/VRM path. This fits the shape
   better: the die is still clamped and reads normal, the memory and PCB read hot, and the gap is flat (card4's
   edge held 77–78 °C across 180 samples, a ±1 band) rather than tracking power.

**The part that matters operationally:** the fan control loop follows **junction**, and card4's junction is fine —
so the loop has no idea its memory is at 95.7 °C, and card4 runs a *lower* fan duty than cards 2-3. Every sensor
does publish its own limits (corrected mid-investigation, because an earlier line in this log said memory
published none — it does, and reading them is what makes the judgement below possible):

| sensor | limit | emergency | card1 | card2 | card3 | card4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `edge` | `temp1_crit` 110 | — | 68.0 | 67.8 | 66.2 | **77.3** |
| `junction` | `temp2_crit` **110** | `temp2_emergency` **115** | 89.7 | **102.4** | 99.4 | 99.1 |
| `mem` (GDDR7) | `temp3_crit` **108** | `temp3_emergency` **113** | 83.9 | 85.2 | 81.6 | **94.4** |

(90 s at 1 Hz, all four at 293-294 W; junction peaks 91 / 105 / 101 / 100, mem peaks 86 / 86 / 84 / 96; **no
sample crossed 105 junction or 100 mem**.) So the Monitor now reads all three **by label**, puts `junction` on the
headline with **its own `crit` as the axis**, gives `mem` its own wave on **its own `crit` (108 — lower than the
die's)**, keeps `edge` in text, and carries the fan (`fan1_input`, `pwm1`) because it is the actuator here.

**Still open, in order of what would settle it:**

1. An idle series with all three sensors per card (confirms "load-dependent, not offset" on mem/edge as it already
   does on edge).
2. Cool-down slopes per card per sensor after the serve stops (a decoupled memory sensor cools slowly).
3. `ipmitool -I open sdr elist` / `sdr type Temperature` under load, for inlet, slot and fan sensors — the SEL
   already shows `TR2/TR3 Temperature` flapping at **88–95 °C against a 90 °C critical** earlier (entity `49.0`,
   unidentified), and PSU2 delivering 0 W (see `incident-2026-11-02/bmc-sensors-watchdog-psu.txt`). Needs the
   operator.
4. Physical: which slot is card4, is a blanking panel missing next to it, and (if 1–3 point at contact) re-pad.
5. A memory-bandwidth-only load on one card at a time (`r9k` memset-style kernel) to see whether card4's mem
   rises faster per byte moved — the cleanest software discriminator, needs the box free.

## 15:0x — is this safe, and would a power cap buy anything? (measured before recommending)

Same instrument, 90 s at 1 Hz, plus the published power and fan envelopes:

| | card1 | card2 | card3 | card4 | published envelope |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| W avg (peak) | 294 (317) | 293 (309) | 293 (318) | 294 (316) | `power1_cap` **300 W** = `power1_cap_default`; range 210-330 |
| junction avg (peak) | 89.0 (91) | **102.3 (105)** | 99.3 (101) | 98.4 (100) | crit 110, emergency 115 |
| mem avg (peak) | 83.9 (86) | 85.2 (86) | 81.6 (84) | **94.4 (96)** | crit 108, emergency 113 |
| fan (rpm = target) | 3969 | 4640 | 4481 | 4435 | `fan1_target` ~4.4-4.6k, **`fan1_max` 5000** |

Three facts that decide it:

1. **The wall is already there.** All four cards average **293-294 W against a 300 W PPT** (`power1_cap` is at its
   vendor default, and this guest installs no cap service). "Uncapped" in the older notes was loose: the
   *average* is pinned at the cap; what exceeds it is short transients (`power1_average` sampling 439-511 W in
   other windows). Lowering `power1_cap` therefore does not buy margin that is being used — it converts headroom
   into slower prefill, which is exactly this workload's bottleneck.
2. **The thermal tax is already being paid automatically.** card2 at **junction ≥ 103 °C ran 2,798 MHz averaged,
   and 3,134 MHz when its junction was under 100** — the DPM is trading ~11% of clock for temperature. A lower
   power wall deepens that trade; it does not avoid it.
3. **The measured price of a cap is not small.** The repo's own 210 W gate numbers against this box at 300 W:
   prefill 5,175 / 7,141 / 7,233 tok/s (2k/8k/32k) against **7,702 / 9,633 / 8,963**, and aggregate at 8 / 16
   streams 530 / 944 against **841 / 1,164**. −25 to −33% of prompt throughput, which is where today's pain is.

**Judgement as measured:** nothing is near a limit — the thinnest margins are card2's junction (peak 105, avg 102
against crit 110) and card4's memory (peak 96 against crit 108), and no sample in any window taken today crossed
them. **Cutting the power wall is the wrong first lever here**: it is a box-wide tax on all four cards (and on
every number in this repo's tables) applied to fix one card's *local* thermal path, and the automatic clock
throttling is already doing the protective job.

**What to do first instead** (both need root; `power1_cap` and `fan1_target` are root-only writes):

- Raise `fan1_target` on **card4** from 4435 toward ~4900 (`fan1_max` is 5000; card2 already runs 4640). Then
  re-read: if card4's mem falls ~5-8 °C, the airflow hypothesis wins; if it barely moves, the memory-side
  interface hypothesis gains. Either way this costs a couple of watts of fan and some noise, and no tokens.
- Raise card2's slightly too, since its junction is the thin one — and watch its sclk: if clocks recover when
  junction falls, that is **free throughput**, not a cost.
- Keep the cap decision for a real trigger: mem sustained over ~103 °C (95% of its crit), or junction ≥106-108
  across the pack, or a correlation between peak draw and the PCIe/reset story. Then cap at **270 W**, not 210,
  and re-measure the prefill ladder — 210 W is the release-gate configuration and its cost is measured above.

The box-level risk in this window is not the GPUs: PSU2 still outputs 0 W (`incident-2026-11-02/bmc-sensors-
watchdog-psu.txt` §5), so everything runs through one supply while four cards transient to >500 W. With the
operator's number -- **the supply is 2600 W** -- that stops being a loading problem and stays a redundancy
problem: 1,176 W of GPU plus ~100-400 W of platform is **~52-62% of one unit at steady state**, ~85-90% on a
worst-case synchronized prefill step, and the chassis' own budget (4 × 300 W PPT = 1,200 W of GPU) is written
to sit on a single 2600 W supply. So a power cap buys nothing electrically either. The one cheap thing on that
list is physical: **is the second cord actually seated, and on which circuit?** (§5b has the arithmetic.)
