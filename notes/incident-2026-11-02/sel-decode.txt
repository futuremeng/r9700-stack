# the raw SEL against the seven deaths -- and why nobody power-cycled this box

Source: `sudo ipmitool -I open sel elist` run on the host by the operator, 2026-11-02 ~00:40 UTC.
`sel info`: 3639 entries, percent used 100%, free 0 bytes, `Overflow: false`, last add
`11/02/2026 12:27:02 AM UTC`, last delete `02/15/2025 03:05:51 AM UTC`.
SEL clock == OS UTC clock (`sel time get` 11/02/2026 12:37:41 == `date -u`).

## 1. the web-UI `ID: 4778..4794` are SEL record ids printed in DECIMAL

`0x12aa` = 4778 and `0x12ba` = 4794, and record `0x12ba` is the last add (`00:27:02`) that `sel info`
reported. That is why `sel get 0x4794` failed: the record the UI calls 4794 is hex `0x12ba`.
Every row of the earlier UI paste maps one-for-one onto the SEL:

The UI renders the sensor name, not the state: seven of those `power off` rows are **Deasserted** --
i.e. the BMC recording that power came *back*, not that it went away. The paste's apparent
"login then, a minute later, a power off" order was just the same pair read newest-first.

## 2. the `Unknown` OEM record is a per-boot record, not a session login

There is exactly one `Unknown | Asserted` per boot in this window (7 boots, 7 records), always 57-65 s
after that boot's power-on record and 26-50 s before the first journal line of that boot. There are **no**
`Unknown` records anywhere in the SEL before 20:36:11 on 11/01 -- not for the clean reboots of 11/01
03:26, not in the whole 10/30-10/31 working session, not in the 2025 body of the log. So it is not a
user-login event: it starts appearing exactly when the deaths start. Reading A in
`bmc-power-events.txt` (someone opened the BMC UI and cycled the hung box) is dead.

Deaths 2, 6 and 7: power is back **9 s, 41 s and 56 s** after the last journal line, with no
power-off record and no human in the loop (death 7: nobody logged in, pi died with it). POST then takes
~90 s to the first journal line. A person cannot be that fast and that consistent; the platform is
resetting itself and the BMC's power-restore policy (`previous`/`always-on` -- still to be confirmed,
see `bmc-sensors-watchdog-psu.txt` §4) brings it back.

Death 3 is two power-on transitions 91 s apart with a single OS boot after them: the platform
re-tried POST on its own. Also not a human gesture.

**Death 4 is the odd one out and the only genuine power-off.** Record `0x12b4` = `Asserted` at
23:54:57 is the only true power-off in the whole window, it lands 1m50 after boot `3b531c63` stopped
logging, and it is the only death whose recovery boot carries **no** reset-reason latch. So death 4 =
the box went fully dark (hung 1m50, then powered off, then back ~23:58 -- that power-on record is
missing, plausibly dropped: the SEL is at 0 bytes free). The other six = live resets of a powered
platform, which is exactly what a data-fabric sync flood looks like, and exactly what the firmware's
own `Previous system reset reason [0x88000800]` says.

## 4. what the whole SEL contains, and does not contain

Body of the log, record ids `0x491`..`0x12ba`, 04/2025 -> 11/02/2026:

- Thousands of `TR2 Temperature` / `TR3 Temperature` `Upper Non-critical going high` (88 C) and
  `Upper Critical going high` (90 C) with readings up to 96 C, plus a handful of `TR1 Temperature`
  37/40 C. **All of them end 06/06/2025.** There is not one thermal record in 2026, so the
  "no thermal event" statements in `notes/bug-mes-invalidate-tlbs.md:156` and `notes/mes-timeouts.md:43`
  stand for their incidents. (The reason they stopped is not that things cooled: today TR2 and TR4 read
  `No Reading` and TR3 reads `0 degrees C`. See §5.)
- `Power Unit PowerUnit | Power off/down` Asserted/Deasserted pairs at each real power transition.
  The 2025 ones are followed by `Pre-Init |<epoch>|` records -- the signature of the BMC itself losing
  its time base, i.e. a full AC loss. None of the seven deaths has a `Pre-Init` record: the BMC stayed
  up and time-stamped everything normally.
- `Version Change PCIE_Hardware | Hardware change detected` repeatedly across 10/31 (01:33, 02:40,
  05:41, 05:44, 06:20) and `Mem_Hardware`+`CPU_Hardware`+`PCIE_Hardware` at 10/31 03:26:14 -- the
  BIOS/hardware working session, matching the boot at 11/01 03:27:54 in `boots.txt`.
- `Physical Security #0xfc | General Chassis intrusion | Asserted` many times, all with `Pre-Init`
  or epoch `03155148xx` stamps, i.e. during that same bench session / BMC restarts. Not new, not
  related to the deaths.
- A run of `Pre-Init` and `01/01/2011`-dated records (ids `0x123d`..`0x1285`) sitting between
  Dec-2025 and 10/30/2026 dated records: the BMC clock was at its factory default for a stretch, with
  `Version Change Mem_Hardware/CPU_Hardware/PCIE_Hardware` at "12:03:15" and a `Drive Slot / Bay
  Backplane1 HD01 | Drive Present` at 05/09/2025. Treat as BMC reflash / CMOC-lost history, not as
  events of this incident.
- **Zero** `Critical Interrupt`/NMI records, **zero** watchdog records, **zero** PSU records
  (`PSU1/PSU2 AC Lost`, `Over Curr`, `Over Temp`, `PWR Detect` never fire anywhere in 3639 entries),
  **zero** voltage/12 V records.

## 5. what is still open

1. `Unknown | Asserted` is un-decoded: `sel elist` gives no sensor type, no entity, no raw bytes for it.
   Get them:
   `sudo ipmitool -I open sel get 0x12ba` and `sudo ipmitool -I open sel get 0x12b4`
   (record type, generator id, event data bytes) -- that names the OEM record and tells us whether the
   BMC is logging a restart cause.
2. The one human question left: `0x12b4` at 23:54:57 (death 4). The web UI **操作日志** page will show
   whether a chassis power command with a username/source IP precedes it. If it does, that one death was
   a manual power cycle; the other six stay platform-initiated either way.
3. The SEL is full (0 bytes free, last delete 02/2025) and at least one power-on record inside the
   incident window is already missing. With the operator's OK:
   `sudo ipmitool -I open sel save $D/sel-full.bin` then `sudo ipmitool -I open sel clear`
   before the next repro, otherwise the next death will also lose records.
4. Sensor health: TR2/TR4 `No Reading`, TR3 `0 degrees C` -- the four `TR*` sensors that produced the
   2025 flap era now report nothing (see §6 of `bmc-sensors-watchdog-psu.txt`), so the BMC has no
   thermal visibility into whatever they watch. `sudo ipmitool -I open sdr get 'TR2 Temperature'` etc.
   to get the entity/labels and confirm they are simply absent rather than failed.
5. PSU2 at 0 W is a real redundancy hole (PSU1 carries all 208 W) but is **not** the cause of these
   six deaths: no power-off record, no AC-lost record, no `Pre-Init` at any of them.

## 6. consequence for the diagnosis

The seven deaths are the platform resetting itself while powered, not power cuts, not a BMC watchdog
(stopped, action `No action`, no expiry flags), not a mains/PSU event (never logged once in 3639
records), not a human. That puts them squarely inside the firmware's own verdict --
`Previous system reset reason [0x88000800]: an uncorrected error caused a data fabric sync flood event`
-- and inside the BERT: `ProcessorError`, `cache error`, `Uncorrected`, socket 0, APIC `0x82` (6x) and
`0x83` (1x), i.e. the same CPU and adjacent APIC ids, six of seven with the identical MCE signature
`Bank 5 aea0000000000108 / SYND 4d000000 / IPID 500b024029a00 / PPIN 2b61290dcb2002b`.
The idle death (7) and the 9 s self-recovery (2) mean load is not required to trigger it.
